How to Set Up Constant Contact: A Step-by-Step Getting Started Guide

Constant Contact Setup Guide Updated March 2026

Constant Contact is a straightforward email marketing tool aimed at small businesses and nonprofits. It has a guided setup process and good customer support, making it a reasonable choice if you want something simple with live help available.

Step-by-step setup guide

Step 1
Create your account
5-10 minutes

Go to constantcontact.com and sign up for a free trial. Enter your business name, industry, and contact details. Constant Contact uses this to tailor your onboarding experience.

Step 2
Authenticate your sending domain
15-30 minutes

Go to Settings, then Email Settings, then Authentication. Follow the instructions to add DKIM records to your DNS provider. Verify once propagated.

Step 3
Create a contact list
5-10 minutes

Go to Contacts and create a new list. Name it clearly. Constant Contact organizes contacts by lists, so plan your list structure before importing.

Step 4
Import your contacts
10-20 minutes

Upload your CSV to your contact list. Map each column to the correct field. Review the import summary before confirming.

Step 5
Set up a signup form
10-15 minutes

Go to Sign-Up Forms and create a new form. Customize the fields and design. Embed the form on your website or use Constant Contact's hosted landing page option.

Step 6
Create a welcome automation
20-30 minutes

Go to Campaigns, then Automated Emails. Create a welcome series triggered when someone joins your list. Write a short welcome email and activate the automation.

Step 7
Send your first email
15-20 minutes

Go to Campaigns and create a new email. Choose a template, write your content, select your list, preview and test, then send or schedule.

Tips for getting the most out of Constant Contact

Things worth knowing before you start
  • Constant Contact's phone support is a genuine differentiator if you are not technically confident - use it
  • The event management features are useful if you run in-person events or webinars
  • Keep your contact lists clean - Constant Contact charges by contact count, so removing inactive subscribers saves money
  • Use the subject line A/B testing to improve open rates over time

The bottom line

Constant Contact is easy to get up and running quickly. It is not the most feature-rich tool, but the simplicity is the point. Get your domain authenticated and your first automation running, then focus on growing your list.
