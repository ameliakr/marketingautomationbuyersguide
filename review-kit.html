Kit Review 2026: Pricing, Features, and Free Plan

Kit Review Updated March 2026

Kit (formerly ConvertKit) was built for one type of user: the online creator. Bloggers, newsletter writers, course sellers, and podcasters are the target audience, and the tool is shaped around their needs rather than adapted from a generic business template.

The result is a tool that does certain things exceptionally well - primarily growing and segmenting an audience, selling digital products, and sending personality-driven emails - while intentionally leaving out features that creators do not need, like complex CRM pipelines or webinar hosting.

If you are a creator, Kit is worth serious consideration. If you are not, it probably is not the right fit.

The free plan: the most generous in the industry

Kit's free plan covers up to 10,000 subscribers with no time limit. This is by far the most generous free tier of any email marketing tool. MailerLite caps free at 1,000. HubSpot caps marketing emails at 2,000 sends per month. Kit lets you grow to 10,000 subscribers, send unlimited broadcast emails, and build basic automation sequences - all for free.

For a creator just starting out, this removes the financial barrier entirely. You can build a real audience before spending a penny.

Audience and segmentation

Kit uses a tags-based system rather than traditional lists, which makes segmenting your audience by interest, behavior, and purchase history cleaner and more flexible. You can tag subscribers based on what they click, what they buy, what forms they fill out, and what automation sequences they have completed. This makes it easy to send highly targeted emails to specific segments without creating duplicate contacts.

Sequences and automation

Kit's sequences tool is designed for drip campaigns - a series of emails sent over time to new subscribers. The interface is clean and the logic is easy to follow. Basic automation is available: you can tag subscribers, add them to sequences, and trigger actions based on their behavior.

What Kit does not have is the complex branching logic and multi-path workflows of ActiveCampaign or even Moosend. If you need automations that split based on behavior and follow different paths, Kit's automation builder will feel limiting. For a creator sending a welcome sequence and a course drip, it is perfectly adequate.

Digital product sales

Kit includes a native commerce feature that lets you sell digital products directly through the platform. You can sell ebooks, courses, templates, and other digital downloads without a separate e-commerce tool. This is unique among email marketing platforms at this price point and is a meaningful feature for creators who sell directly to their audience.

Email design

Kit leans toward plain-text style emails rather than heavily designed HTML newsletters. This reflects the creator use case - personal, conversational emails that feel like they came from a person rather than a brand. If you want polished, visually complex email templates, Kit's editor is not designed for that. If you want clean, readable, personal emails, it is excellent.

Kit is right for you if...

  • You are a blogger, newsletter writer, podcaster, or course creator
  • You want to grow an audience for free up to 10,000 subscribers
  • You sell digital products and want email and commerce in one place
  • You prefer personal, text-based emails over heavily designed templates
  • You want clean, simple segmentation based on tags and subscriber behavior

Kit is not right for you if...

  • You run an e-commerce store - Klaviyo or Omnisend are built for that
  • You need complex automation with multiple branches and conditions
  • You need a built-in CRM or deal pipeline
  • You want SMS marketing alongside email
  • You are a B2B company with a sales team - this tool is built for creators, not sales teams

The bottom line

Kit is the best email tool for creators, full stop. The 10,000 subscriber free plan is an extraordinary offer, the tag-based segmentation is clean and flexible, and the digital product commerce feature is genuinely useful. If you are a creator, it is hard to make a case for starting anywhere else. If you are not a creator, it is probably not the right tool for you.
