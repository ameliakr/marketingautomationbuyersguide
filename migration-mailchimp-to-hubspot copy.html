How to Migrate from Mailchimp to HubSpot

Mailchimp HubSpot Migration Updated March 2026

Migrating from Mailchimp to HubSpot is the right move when you have outgrown standalone email marketing and need a platform that connects your marketing and sales data. HubSpot's free CRM alone is more capable than anything Mailchimp offers at the contact management level.

The migration itself is straightforward for contacts and lists. The more significant shift is mental - HubSpot organizes data differently than Mailchimp, and learning the HubSpot way of doing things takes a few days. Budget time for that transition, not just the technical move.

This guide walks you through every step.

Step-by-step migration guide

Step 1
Export your contacts from Mailchimp
15-30 minutes

Go to Audience in Mailchimp and export your full contact list as a CSV. Include all merge fields and tags. Export your unsubscribe and bounce lists separately so you can suppress them in HubSpot.

Step 2
Document your automations
15-60 minutes

Screenshot or write down every active automation in Mailchimp - the trigger, conditions, emails, and timing. You will not have access to this once your Mailchimp account is closed, so do this before you cancel.

Step 3
Export your email templates
30-60 minutes

Save the HTML of any email templates you want to reuse. You can import HTML into HubSpot or rebuild templates using their editor.

Step 4
Download your campaign reports
10-15 minutes

Export your historical performance data from Mailchimp. Open rates, click rates, and engagement history will not carry over to HubSpot and you lose access once your account is closed.

Step 5
Set up your HubSpot account
30-60 minutes

Complete your account setup in HubSpot. Authenticate your sending domain by adding SPF and DKIM records to your DNS settings. This is the most important step for deliverability. HubSpot will guide you through it during setup.

Step 6
Import your contacts
20-45 minutes

Import your CSV into HubSpot and map each column to the correct field. Import your suppression list separately to prevent emailing contacts who have opted out. Review the import report and fix any errors before moving on.

Step 7
Rebuild your automations
1-3 hours

Use HubSpot's automation builder to recreate your workflows. Start with your highest-priority sequences and test each one with a test subscriber before activating.

Step 8
Update your signup forms
30-60 minutes

Replace your Mailchimp forms with HubSpot forms on your website. Update any integrations, popups, or third-party tools that were connected to Mailchimp.

Step 9
Test everything before going live
1-2 hours

Send test emails to yourself. Trigger each automation manually and confirm it fires correctly. Check all links, verify unsubscribe works, and confirm domain authentication is passing. Do not cancel Mailchimp until this is done.

Step 10
Cancel Mailchimp
5 minutes

Once HubSpot is fully set up and tested, cancel your Mailchimp account. Check your billing date first to avoid being charged for another month.

Before you cancel Mailchimp

Export your contact list, suppression list, campaign history, and templates. Update all connected integrations to point to HubSpot. Once your Mailchimp account is closed, this data is gone.

Key differences to understand before switching

HubSpot uses lists differently. Mailchimp organizes contacts by Audience. HubSpot organizes contacts by lists and properties, with no concept of separate audiences. All contacts are in one database, segmented by properties and list membership. This is more flexible but requires remapping your mental model.

The free plan has email send limits. HubSpot's free plan limits you to 2,000 marketing email sends per month. If your list is larger or you send frequently, you will need a paid plan or to use HubSpot primarily for CRM and use another tool for bulk email.

Automation depth depends on your plan. Basic sequences are available on free. The full workflow builder with branching logic requires Marketing Hub Professional, which starts at $800/mo. If email automation is your primary need, consider whether ActiveCampaign offers more at a lower price point.

Why people switch from Mailchimp to HubSpot

The most common reason is needing a CRM. Mailchimp is a marketing tool. HubSpot is a marketing and sales platform. If your team needs pipeline visibility, lead scoring, and contact management alongside email campaigns, HubSpot handles all of it natively. The free CRM tier covers most small business needs at no cost.

The bottom line

Migrating from Mailchimp to HubSpot is a half-day project for most businesses. Your contacts move over cleanly. The work is in rebuilding your automations and updating your forms. Keep both accounts running in parallel until you have confirmed everything works in HubSpot, then cancel Mailchimp.
