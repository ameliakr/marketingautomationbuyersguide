ActiveCampaign Review 2026: Pricing, Features, and Automation Depth

ActiveCampaign Review SMB Updated March 2026

ActiveCampaign is the tool most people graduate to when they outgrow basic email marketing. It sits at a specific intersection: more powerful than Mailchimp or MailerLite, more affordable than HubSpot, and more automation-focused than almost anything else at its price point.

The tradeoff is complexity. ActiveCampaign is not a tool you can set up in an afternoon. It has a learning curve, and if you do not need what it offers, you will spend money on features you never use.

This review covers what ActiveCampaign does well, where it falls short, who it is right for, and whether the price is justified.

Automation: the best in its class

ActiveCampaign's automation builder is the main reason people choose it over everything else. You can build multi-step workflows triggered by almost any action - a form submission, a page visit, a purchase, a tag being added, a custom event, or a date. Conditions split contacts into different paths based on their behavior. Goals end automations early when a contact achieves a desired outcome. Wait steps, A/B splits, and predictive sending are all available on mid-tier plans.

This is not just a welcome sequence builder. You can build the kind of sophisticated nurture flows that used to require enterprise tools. For a business running a complex lead nurture program, a SaaS onboarding sequence, or a multi-stage e-commerce re-engagement campaign, ActiveCampaign does things that tools like MailerLite simply cannot.

The catch is that building these workflows takes time. If you want a simple welcome email, you do not need ActiveCampaign. But if you want to trigger different sequences based on what a contact clicks, what pages they visit, or what they buy, you will not find a better tool at this price.

CRM: capable but not a dedicated sales tool

ActiveCampaign includes a built-in CRM with deal pipelines, contact and deal scoring, task management, and sales automation. For a small team where marketing and sales overlap, this is a real advantage - you can manage the full customer journey from first touch to closed deal without leaving the platform.

It is not as deep as a dedicated CRM like Salesforce or even HubSpot's CRM. The pipeline view is functional rather than elegant, and the reporting on the CRM side is more limited than the marketing reporting. But for a team of one to ten people who need basic pipeline management alongside their email automation, it is more than sufficient.

Pricing

ActiveCampaign starts at $15/mo for up to 1,000 contacts on the Starter plan. This includes email marketing, basic automation, and limited CRM features. The Plus plan at $49/mo adds full CRM functionality, SMS, and advanced automation. Professional starts at $79/mo and adds predictive sending, attribution reporting, and site messaging.

Pricing scales with contact count, which is standard across the industry. At 10,000 contacts, you are looking at roughly $70/mo on Starter and $125/mo on Plus. For what you get - specifically the automation depth and CRM - these are fair prices. You would pay significantly more for comparable functionality from HubSpot or Salesforce.

The absence of a free plan is a genuine downside. You get a 14-day free trial, which is enough to evaluate the platform, but you cannot grow into it for free the way you can with MailerLite or HubSpot.

Ease of use

ActiveCampaign is not beginner-friendly. The interface has improved significantly over the years, but the sheer number of features means there is a real learning curve. Expect to spend a few hours getting oriented, and a few more building your first serious automation. The onboarding is guided and the help documentation is thorough, but this is not a tool you pick up in an afternoon.

If you need something you can set up quickly and do not plan to use advanced features, look at Moosend or MailerLite instead.

Deliverability

ActiveCampaign has strong deliverability. They maintain good sender reputation through strict list management policies and provide dedicated IP options on higher-tier plans for high-volume senders. For most small to mid-size businesses, shared IPs perform well. Inbox placement rates are consistently good across major email clients.

Support

Live chat support is available on all paid plans. Response times are generally fast, and the support team has good product knowledge. There is also a large library of documentation, video tutorials, and a community forum. The onboarding experience for new accounts is among the better ones in the industry.

What ActiveCampaign does not do well

The email template builder is functional but not as polished as Mailchimp's or MailerLite's. If email design is a priority, you may find it limiting. The mobile app is basic. Reporting is strong on the marketing side but lighter on the CRM side. And the pricing can add up quickly if you are on a higher-tier plan with a large contact list.

ActiveCampaign is right for you if...

  • You need automation that goes beyond a basic welcome series
  • You want CRM and email marketing in one place
  • You are in B2B or run a service business with a longer sales cycle
  • You are coming from Mailchimp and have hit its automation limits
  • You are comfortable with a learning curve in exchange for more power

ActiveCampaign is not right for you if...

  • You just need to send newsletters and simple campaigns
  • You want something you can set up in a few hours
  • Budget is tight and you need a free plan to start
  • You run an e-commerce store where Klaviyo or Omnisend would be a better fit
  • You need deep enterprise CRM features - HubSpot goes further on that front

The bottom line

ActiveCampaign earns its reputation. The automation builder is genuinely best-in-class at its price point, the CRM is a real asset for service businesses and B2B teams, and the deliverability is strong. The learning curve and lack of a free plan are real drawbacks, but for a business that needs what ActiveCampaign offers, it is hard to beat.
