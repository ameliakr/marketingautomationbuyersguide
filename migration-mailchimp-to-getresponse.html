How to Migrate from Mailchimp to GetResponse

Mailchimp GetResponse Migration Updated March 2026

GetResponse is one of the more feature-packed alternatives to Mailchimp at a similar price point. It includes email marketing, automation, landing pages, webinars, and SMS in one platform - more than Mailchimp offers at equivalent tiers.

The migration process is standard for a platform switch of this type. Contacts export and import cleanly. Automations need to be rebuilt. The GetResponse interface takes a bit of time to get oriented in, but it is well-documented and the support team is responsive.

This guide walks you through the full process.

Step-by-step migration guide

Step 1
Export your contacts from Mailchimp
15-30 minutes

Go to Audience in Mailchimp and export your full contact list as a CSV. Include all merge fields and tags. Export your unsubscribe and bounce lists separately so you can suppress them in GetResponse.

Step 2
Document your automations
15-60 minutes

Screenshot or write down every active automation in Mailchimp - the trigger, conditions, emails, and timing. You will not have access to this once your Mailchimp account is closed, so do this before you cancel.

Step 3
Export your email templates
30-60 minutes

Save the HTML of any email templates you want to reuse. You can import HTML into GetResponse or rebuild templates using their editor.

Step 4
Download your campaign reports
10-15 minutes

Export your historical performance data from Mailchimp. Open rates, click rates, and engagement history will not carry over to GetResponse and you lose access once your account is closed.

Step 5
Set up your GetResponse account
30-60 minutes

Complete your account setup in GetResponse. Authenticate your sending domain by adding SPF and DKIM records to your DNS settings. This is the most important step for deliverability. GetResponse will guide you through it during setup.

Step 6
Import your contacts
20-45 minutes

Import your CSV into GetResponse and map each column to the correct field. Import your suppression list separately to prevent emailing contacts who have opted out. Review the import report and fix any errors before moving on.

Step 7
Rebuild your automations
1-3 hours

Use GetResponse's automation builder to recreate your workflows. Start with your highest-priority sequences and test each one with a test subscriber before activating.

Step 8
Update your signup forms
30-60 minutes

Replace your Mailchimp forms with GetResponse forms on your website. Update any integrations, popups, or third-party tools that were connected to Mailchimp.

Step 9
Test everything before going live
1-2 hours

Send test emails to yourself. Trigger each automation manually and confirm it fires correctly. Check all links, verify unsubscribe works, and confirm domain authentication is passing. Do not cancel Mailchimp until this is done.

Step 10
Cancel Mailchimp
5 minutes

Once GetResponse is fully set up and tested, cancel your Mailchimp account. Check your billing date first to avoid being charged for another month.

Before you cancel Mailchimp

Export your contact list, suppression list, campaign history, and templates. Update all connected integrations to point to GetResponse. Once your Mailchimp account is closed, this data is gone.

What GetResponse does differently from Mailchimp

Webinars are built in. GetResponse includes a webinar tool on paid plans. If you run online events, this eliminates the need for a separate webinar platform. It is not as full-featured as dedicated tools like Zoom Webinars, but for basic events it works well.

The automation builder is more visual. GetResponse's workflow builder uses a canvas interface similar to ActiveCampaign's. It supports more complex sequences than Mailchimp's Customer Journeys, with more trigger options and branching logic.

Landing pages are included on all plans. GetResponse has a solid landing page builder that works across all tiers including free. If you were paying for a separate landing page tool with Mailchimp, you can consolidate.

Why people switch from Mailchimp to GetResponse

The most common reasons are pricing and feature breadth. GetResponse offers a free plan for up to 500 contacts, and paid plans that include webinars and SMS that Mailchimp charges significantly more for. If you run webinars or want email and SMS in one platform without paying for two tools, GetResponse covers it at a competitive price.

The bottom line

Migrating from Mailchimp to GetResponse is a half-day project for most businesses. Your contacts move over cleanly. The work is in rebuilding your automations and updating your forms. Keep both accounts running in parallel until you have confirmed everything works in GetResponse, then cancel Mailchimp.
