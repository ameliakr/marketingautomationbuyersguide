Keap Review 2026: Pricing, Features, and CRM Capabilities

Keap Review Updated March 2026

Keap (formerly Infusionsoft) is not an email marketing tool. It is a full business management platform for service businesses: CRM, email automation, invoicing, payments, appointment booking, and pipeline management all under one roof. The price reflects that, starting at $249/mo, it is among the most expensive tools on this list by a wide margin.

The audience is narrow but specific: coaches, consultants, agencies, and service businesses that need to manage complex client relationships, automate follow-up sequences, and handle payments without juggling five separate tools. For that audience, Keap can be worth the price. For everyone else, it is significant overkill.

What Keap is built for

Keap's core use case is the service business with a complex sales and delivery process. A business coach who needs to capture leads, nurture them with automated sequences, book discovery calls, send proposals, collect payment, onboard new clients, and follow up throughout the engagement: Keap can automate the entire lifecycle in one platform.

The CRM includes detailed contact records, custom fields, tags, and pipeline stages. The automation builder triggers actions based on almost any event, a form submission, a payment, a tag added, a date, or a contact reaching a specific pipeline stage. Combined with built-in invoicing and payment collection, it eliminates the need for separate tools like a CRM, email platform, invoicing software, and appointment scheduler.

Pricing: high but potentially justified

Keap starts at $249/mo for up to 1,500 contacts. This is significantly more than any other tool in this guide. The justification is consolidation. If Keap replaces a CRM at $50/mo, an email tool at $50/mo, invoicing software at $30/mo, and an appointment booking tool at $20/mo, the math starts to work. If you are not replacing multiple tools, it does not.

Ease of use

Keap has a meaningful learning curve. The platform is powerful but complex, and onboarding takes more than a few hours. Keap offers dedicated onboarding support for new customers, which helps significantly, but this is not a tool you set up in a weekend. Budget time to learn it properly or you will not use most of what you are paying for.

Who should not use Keap

If you primarily need email marketing and do not need CRM, invoicing, and payment features, Keap is expensive overkill. ActiveCampaign at $49/mo gives you strong CRM and automation at a fraction of the price. HubSpot's free plan covers basic CRM at no cost. Keap's price only makes sense when you are actively using its full suite of features.

Keap is right for you if...

  • You run a coaching, consulting, or agency business with a complex client pipeline
  • You want CRM, email automation, invoicing, and payments in a single platform
  • You are currently paying for multiple separate tools that Keap would consolidate
  • You need sophisticated automation triggered by client actions and pipeline stages
  • Budget is not the primary concern and you need a platform that covers your full operation

Keap is not right for you if...

  • You primarily need email marketing, the price is not justified for email alone
  • You are a small business just getting started, the cost and complexity are too high
  • You run an e-commerce store. Klaviyo or Omnisend are built for that
  • You need something easy to set up quickly, the learning curve is significant
  • Your budget is under $100/mo, there are better-value tools at every lower price point

The bottom line

Keap is the right platform for service businesses that have outgrown a patchwork of separate tools and need everything managed in one place. The price is high, but for the right business it eliminates real costs and operational friction. For everyone else, it is more tool than you need.
